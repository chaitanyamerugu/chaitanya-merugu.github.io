Opening the black box: explainable AI for large language models

Models such as GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Llama now help with medical, financial, and legal decisions, yet nobody can fully explain how they reach an answer. This explainer covers what explainability means, why it is hard, how validation and metrics show whether a model can be trusted, and what the major labs are doing about it. It includes two hands-on demos.

Definition

Explainable AI (XAI) is the set of methods that let people understand why an AI system produced a particular output, and decide how far to trust it.

Input→Model (billions of parameters)→Output+Explanation
Why transparency is critical
1Trust: people rely on a model appropriately only when they understand its reasoning and its limits.
2Safety: explanations expose errors, bias, and shortcuts before they cause harm.
3Accountability: laws in finance, healthcare, and the EU increasingly require decisions to be explained.
4Improvement: engineers can only fix what they can see.

Patients & customers

“Why was my loan denied?” They need a plain-language reason they can act on.

Doctors, lawyers, analysts

“Should I act on this?” They need to know the evidence behind the answer and how confident the model is.

Engineers & researchers

“What is the model actually computing?” They need internal mechanisms for debugging.

Regulators & auditors

“Is it fair, safe, and compliant?” They need documentation, test results, and audit trails.

Part 1 · Challenges in explainability

Why LLMs are so hard to explain

An LLM’s “reasoning” is spread across billions of numbers that interact in non-linear ways. No single part holds the answer, and each of the obstacles below makes simple explanations unreliable.

Scale & complexity

Decisions emerge from interactions among billions of parameters across dozens of layers, so no single component explains an output.

405 billion
parameters in Meta’s Llama 3.1 405B, one of the largest open-weight models

Superposition

Single neurons are “polysemantic”: one neuron can respond to unrelated concepts, so inspecting neurons one at a time is misleading.

34 million
concepts (“features”) Anthropic extracted from one layer of Claude 3 Sonnet, 2024

Unfaithful explanations

When asked, a model produces a plausible explanation after the fact, and it may not describe how the answer was really computed.

25%
of the time Claude 3.7 Sonnet mentioned a hint it had used to reach its answer (Anthropic, 2025)

Attention isn’t the whole story

Attention heatmaps show where a model “looked”, but researchers have shown that different attention patterns can give the same prediction. Attention alone is not a reliable explanation.

Jain & Wallace
“Attention is not Explanation,” NAACL 2019

Data quality & bias

Models absorb the gaps and biases in trillions of training tokens, and most labs do not fully disclose that data, so the source of a biased output is hard to trace.

15.6 trillion
training tokens for Llama 3.1, far too many for any human to review

Regulation & accountability

Laws require explanations that today’s tools cannot always give, and closed models limit what outside auditors can inspect.

Closed weights
GPT and Claude internals can be studied only by their developers
The regulatory landscape

EU AI Act (2024)

Transparency, documentation, and human-oversight duties for general-purpose and high-risk AI, phased in from 2025.

GDPR, Art. 22 & 13–15

People subject to automated decisions are entitled to “meaningful information about the logic involved.”

US credit law (ECOA)

The CFPB says lenders must give specific reasons for a denial, even if a complex algorithm made the decision (Circular 2022-03).

NIST AI RMF 1.0 (2023)

Lists “explainable and interpretable” as one of seven characteristics of trustworthy AI.

The model explains itself, step by step

Used in: OpenAI o-series, Claude extended thinking, Gemini thinking models
  1. Chest pain that radiates to the left arm is a classic sign of a heart attack.
  2. Shortness of breath and sweating point the same way.
  3. Age 58 and a smoking history increase the risk.
  4. Conclusion: treat as urgent and rule out a heart attack first.
Limitation: the stated reasoning reads well but may not be faithful. Studies by Anthropic found models often leave out factors that actually drove their answer.

What would change the answer?

Methods: counterfactual tests, ablation, input perturbation
Remove “radiating to left arm”92% → 64%
Remove “shortness of breath”92% → 78%
Change age 58 → 2592% → 83%
Change patient sex (bias check)92% → 91%
Limitation: counterfactuals are intuitive and good for spotting bias, but testing many edits is slow, and the edited inputs can look unrealistic to the model.

Takeaway: no single method is complete. Practitioners combine several, and treat a model’s own explanation as a claim to verify, not as ground truth.

Part 2 · Validation & performance metrics

Trust is measured, not assumed

Explanations show why a model answered. Validation shows how often it is right, for whom, and under what conditions. Try the demo below: moving the decision threshold changes which mistakes the model makes.

Hold-out testing

Score the model on data it never saw in training. Use separate validation and test sets, or k-fold cross-validation.

Benchmarks

Standard tests such as MMLU (knowledge), TruthfulQA (honesty), and HELM, which scores 7 metrics including calibration and fairness.

Subgroup & bias audits

Compare error rates across age, sex, language, and other groups. BBQ tests for social bias in answers.

Red-teaming & human evaluation

Experts probe for harms. Human raters judge quality; Chatbot Arena ranks models by blind human votes.

Production monitoring

Track accuracy, drift, hallucination reports, and user feedback after launch, and re-validate when data changes.

Key performance metrics, what they measure, and why they matter for trust
MetricWhat it measuresWhy it matters for trust
AccuracyShare of all predictions that are correctEasy to understand, but misleading when one outcome is rare
PrecisionOf the cases flagged, how many were realLow precision means false alarms, and users start to ignore the model
Recall (sensitivity)Of the real cases, how many were caughtCritical when a miss is dangerous, as in medicine or fraud detection
F1 scoreBalance of precision and recallA single summary number when both kinds of error matter
AUC-ROCRanking quality across all thresholdsCompares models independently of where the threshold is set
Calibration (ECE)Whether “90% confident” is right 90% of the timeConfidence that users can rely on. OpenAI found RLHF reduced GPT-4’s calibration
Hallucination rateShare of answers with made-up factsA core LLM reliability metric, tested with fact-checked question sets
Fairness gapDifference in error rates between groupsShows whether the model is reliable for everyone, not only on average
Part 3 · Current solutions & techniques

From explaining outputs to reading the model’s mind

Techniques range from documentation anyone can read to research tools that trace computations inside the network. The bars show how deeply each one looks into the model.

Documentation

Model & system cards

Published reports on training data, intended use, test results, and known limits. The concept was introduced by Google researchers in 2019 and is now standard across labs.

Looks at: the whole system
Output-level

Attribution & counterfactuals

Integrated Gradients, SHAP, and LIME score which inputs drove a prediction. Google Cloud’s Vertex AI and Meta’s Captum provide them.

Looks at: inputs → output
Reasoning-level

Chain-of-thought & monitoring

Reasoning models show their steps. In 2025, researchers from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, and Meta jointly called for keeping that reasoning monitorable.

Looks at: stated reasoning
Mechanism-level

Mechanistic interpretability

Probes, sparse autoencoders, and circuit tracing find the concepts a network represents and how it combines them.

Looks at: internal computation
Anthropic · Claude

Mapping concepts and tracing thoughts

  • 2024Scaling Monosemanticity: found millions of interpretable features in Claude 3 Sonnet. Amplifying one feature produced “Golden Gate Claude.”
  • 2025Circuit tracing: attribution graphs showed Claude 3.5 Haiku planning rhymes ahead and chaining facts. The tools were open-sourced.
  • 2025Faithfulness research: measured how often reasoning models omit what really drove their answers.
OpenAI · GPT

Using AI to explain AI

  • 2023Automated neuron explanations: GPT-4 wrote and scored explanations for all 307,200 neurons in GPT-2.
  • 2024Sparse autoencoders: extracted 16 million features from GPT-4 and released the training code.
  • 2025Chain-of-thought monitoring: a second model reads the reasoning to catch reward hacking and misbehavior.
Google & Google DeepMind · Gemini

Open tools for the research community

  • 2017Integrated Gradients: a widely used attribution method developed by Google researchers.
  • 2020Learning Interpretability Tool (LIT): a visual tool for exploring NLP model behavior.
  • 2024Gemma Scope: over 400 open sparse autoencoders covering every layer of Gemma 2.
Meta · Llama

Openness as transparency

  • 2019Captum: an open-source PyTorch library for attribution and interpretability.
  • 2023+Open-weight Llama models: let independent researchers inspect internals that closed models do not expose.
  • 2023+Detailed papers and model cards: publish data size, GPU-hours, emissions, and safety evaluations, alongside Llama Guard safety classifiers.
Part 4 · Bringing it together

What builds trust in AI

ExplainabilityWhy did it decide?
+
ValidationDoes it hold up on new data and for every group?
+
MetricsHow often, and in what way, is it wrong?
=
Trustworthy AIUsers can rely on it appropriately
01

Match the explanation to the audience. A patient needs a reason, a doctor needs evidence and confidence, and an auditor needs documentation.

02

Verify explanations; don’t just accept them. A model’s self-explanation may be unfaithful. Cross-check it with attribution and counterfactual tests.

03

Choose metrics based on what an error costs. In triage a miss is worse than a false alarm, so prioritize recall. In spam filtering, prioritize precision.

04

Validate continuously. Benchmarks at launch are not enough. Monitor for drift, bias, and hallucinations in production.
